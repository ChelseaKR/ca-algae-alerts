# i18n status: Applies — English published, Spanish in progress (off)

INTERNATIONALIZATION-STANDARD §1 allows N/A only when all three of its
out-of-scope conditions hold. Two fail here: the site and the app show
natural-language text to the public (condition a) and show dates (condition
c). So the standard applies. Both surfaces publish English only. Spanish
groundwork is built and switched off (docs/DECISIONS.md 0025).

## Current state

- Site: every published page renders `<html lang="en">` from
  `pipeline/src/caalgae/templates/base.html.jinja`. Status sentences live in
  `pipeline/src/caalgae/wording.py`; most page strings are inline in the
  templates. Dates are formatted in English (`wording.date_label`, "Oct 9,
  2026").
- Spanish (off): `pipeline/src/caalgae/i18n.py` holds the English catalog
  (read from `wording.py`), the gate and the rendered-page check;
  `pipeline/src/caalgae/locales/es.json` and `es.status.json` hold the Spanish
  strings and a status per string (`state`, `reviewed`, `same`, `draft`).
  `levels.html.jinja` renders /levels/ and /es/niveles/ through
  `_base_i18n.html.jinja`. Spanish dates come from a month table
  (`i18n.es_date`, "9 de octubre de 2026"). `i18n.LEVELS_ENABLED` and
  `i18n.ES_ENABLED` are False, so neither page is built.
- iOS app: `CFBundleDevelopmentRegion` is `en`. Status sentences live in
  `ios/AlgaeCore/Sources/AlgaeCore/StatusWording.swift`, which mirrors
  `wording.py`; view strings are SwiftUI literals, with no
  `Localizable.xcstrings` yet. Out of scope for now.
- The snapshot carries the state's own water names, spot descriptions and
  advisory levels, verbatim. It carries no UI strings. Its names are the
  rendered-page check's proper-noun allowlist.

## Gate

`make i18n` (inside `make verify`) runs `python -m caalgae.i18n`. With
Spanish off it checks shape, parity, equal-means-declared and state-quote
hashes. `--enabled` shows what turning Spanish on needs; on 2026-11-05 that is
26 problems (22 draft safety strings and 4 sign placeholders).

## Before Spanish is turned on

1. The owner pastes the state's Spanish sign wording from a browser
   (mywaterquality.ca.gov admits only Googlebot) and marks each `state`.
2. A reviewer signs off every safety string (`reviewed`, initials and date).
3. `_ga4.html.jinja`'s opt-out messages come from the catalog.
4. hreflang on both twins, after the ~2026-11-25 read of the dated titles.
